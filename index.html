Example Dental Practice

Illustrative audit excerpt · hypothetical findings, not measured client results

FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION
01

The hero image delays the main content.

Example evidence: A page inspection could reveal a large, full-resolution image being sent to a small mobile screen.

Why it matters: Visitors may wait longer to see treatment information and the appointment action.

Priority: high · Effort: small–medium

Recommended fix: Resize and compress the image, use responsive formats, and prioritize the visible hero rather than lazy-loading it.

How to measure: Compare like-for-like mobile lab tests, then review real-user loading data and appointment actions if available.

02

The mobile call button is hard to find.

Example evidence: A mobile walkthrough could show the phone link only at the bottom of a long page.

Why it matters: A visitor ready to ask about treatment has to search for a way to call.

Priority: high · Effort: small

Recommended fix: Put a clearly labeled, accessible call action near relevant treatment information without obstructing the page.

How to measure: Track call-link taps and, with appropriate consent and tools, distinguish taps from connected, qualified calls.

03

The inquiry form asks too much, too soon.

Example evidence: A form review could identify mandatory fields that aren't needed to arrange an initial conversation.

Why it matters: Extra effort can make an otherwise simple appointment request feel difficult.

Priority: medium · Effort: medium

Recommended fix: Agree the minimum useful fields with the practice. Keep sensitive medical information out of a general inquiry form.

How to measure: Compare form starts, confirmed submissions, and lead quality—not just button clicks.

04

Confirmed bookings need a reliable event.

Example evidence: An authorized analytics test could find that a successful test booking does not produce the agreed event.

Why it matters: The practice cannot reliably connect website activity to appointment requests.

Priority: high · Effort: medium

Recommended fix: Record a consent-aware event only after booking confirmation, deduplicate it, and exclude patient details.

How to measure: Verify test events against actual booking confirmations. This requires authorized access; a public scan cannot establish it.

05

A third-party widget interrupts interaction.

Example evidence: A repeatable lab trace could identify long main-thread tasks associated with a nonessential widget.

Why it matters: The page may respond slowly while someone is trying to navigate or book.

Priority: medium · Effort: medium

Recommended fix: Assess the widget's business value. Remove, defer, or load it on demand, then check that key functions still work.

How to measure: Repeat interaction tests and review real-user responsiveness where available. Faster interaction alone doesn't prove more bookings.

All five examples are fictional. Priorities and effort are illustrative, not a diagnosis or quote for your website. We distinguish observed facts, potential issues, and checks that need authorized access.

Good questions

A little clarity before we start.

What exactly does Frictionless do?

We diagnose website performance, usability, conversion, and measurement problems; prioritize useful fixes; and offer implementation and follow-up measurement. The aim is to get more value from the traffic you already have.

How does the free initial review work?

Email your public website URL and what you'd like to improve. We'll review the request and agree the next step. This is an initial assessment, not a full audit. For an immediate automated performance check, use the Google PageSpeed Insights tool below; it does not send us a request.

Will you tell me what's wrong with my website?

We'll explain what we can actually observe, what looks like a potential issue, and what needs deeper review or authorized access. We won't pretend a public scan can explain actual visitor behavior or establish whether your analytics is working.

Can you fix the problems, or only provide a report?

Implementation is available under a separate agreed scope. We'll discuss access, platform constraints, cost, risk, and verification before making changes.

Do I need to rebuild my website?

Not necessarily. We look for targeted improvements to your existing site first. If a larger rebuild appears justified, we'll explain the evidence and tradeoffs rather than assume one is required.

Can you work with WordPress, Webflow, Shopify, and custom sites?

Public-page review can apply to all of these. What we can implement depends on your platform, theme, hosting, permissions, and third-party tools. We'll confirm feasibility before agreeing the work.

Can you help with Google Analytics and conversion tracking?

Yes. We can review your existing setup or help configure Google Analytics 4 if tracking is missing, using an owner-approved property and consent controls. We help you understand engagement, bounce rates, and meaningful actions such as confirmed inquiries and bookings. Setup and verification require appropriate account access; a public scan alone cannot show actual visitor behavior.

Will you need access to my website or analytics?

Not for the initial public-page review. Deeper measurement work and implementation may require authorized access. We'll ask only for necessary permissions through named, revocable invitations—not passwords sent by email.

How long does an audit take?

Timing depends on the number of pages, journeys, access requirements, and agreed depth. We'll confirm scope and a realistic timeline before starting paid work. The free review doesn't reserve a delivery date.

How much does it cost?

The initial website review is free. Comprehensive audits, implementation, and ongoing optimization are quoted separately after understanding your needs. No paid work starts without an agreed scope and price.

Can you guarantee more leads or sales?

No. Outcomes depend on your traffic, audience, offer, measurement, and implementation. We promise a clear process, evidence-led recommendations, and agreed work focused on measurable customer actions—not a predetermined revenue or conversion increase.

How do you handle access and business data?

Use least-privilege, revocable access for agreed work. Don't email passwords, medical details, legal case information, or other sensitive customer data. We discuss any required data access and handling before the engagement begins.
